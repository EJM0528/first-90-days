Scaling Product's operating system, one rhythm at a time.

How I'd spend the first 90 days in the Chief of Staff to the SVP of Product role, based on what's in the posting.
Erin J. Maloney  |  erin.james13@gmail.com  |  linkedin.com/in/erinjmaloney  |  Austin, TX

This isn't a claim that I already know Affirm's Product org from the inside. It's a plan for how I would spend the first 90 days once I did. Listen before redesigning anything. Fix the handful of things that matter most. Prove those fixes hold at scale. I have built every operating system in my career the same way: start from what the people running the org actually need, not from a template I brought in.

Days 1 – 30: Listen & Diagnose

  • Run a listening tour with the SVP, HR Business Partners, Technology Operations, Finance, and each Product Pillar Head, focused on hidden friction, single points of failure, and information bottlenecks.
  • Secure read only access to roadmaps, sprint tracking, and product analytics dashboards. Review the last two quarters of strategy documents and OKRs, including where AI enablement already stands.
  • Partner with HR to baseline the talent funnel: hiring velocity, interview to offer ratios, performance calibration. Identify where hiring gets stuck, and why.
  • Audit existing all hands scripts, written updates, and executive presentations to understand the tone and priorities already in place before proposing changes.
What I'd have by day 30: A short diagnosis for the SVP naming where decisions stall today, and one high impact fix to start month two.

Days 31 – 60: Design & Pilot

  • Redesign the one or two rhythms causing the most friction: real pre read templates, an agenda built around decisions rather than updates, and a defined way to capture and track follow through.
  • Replace the lowest value alignment meetings with a short written weekly update on product delivery health.
  • Partner with Finance and HR to build a simple tracking system for quarterly reforecasts and hire prioritization tradeoffs.
  • Build the first version of a communication and partner outreach playbook that triggers automatically around product launches: what gets communicated, to whom, and on what timeline before and after launch.
  • Pilot one AI powered workflow in the product lifecycle, such as PRD drafting, competitive analysis, or customer feedback synthesis, and begin drafting the syllabus for the first internal AI session based on what the pilot proves out.
What I'd have by day 60: A single system that pulls headcount, OKR progress, and cross functional dependencies together, so decisions do not wait on someone finding the right spreadsheet.

Days 61 – 90: Scale & Prove

  • Run the first internal AI session for leadership, built on what the pilot actually proved out.
  • Partner with Technology Operations to own the next OKR and roadmap cycle, aligning product priorities with finance, design, engineering, and revenue.
  • Deploy a continuous org health pulse covering engagement, manager effectiveness, and attrition risk, catching leveling drift or leadership gaps before they slow shipping.
  • Take direct ownership of one clearly cross functional bet, most likely getting AI enabled processes embedded across product pillars.
  • Roll the communication and partner outreach playbook out across the full leadership team, and publish a first written narrative piece with PR and comms, a smaller rep before board level writing.
  • Define the boundaries of the role clearly: what I own outright, what needs a quick check with the SVP first, and what stays fully theirs.
What I'd have by day 90: A repeatable operating system covering planning, communications, partner outreach, talent, and cross functional risk, something the org can run on without me re explaining it every quarter.

Why I think this approach works

I used this same approach to build a regulatory operating system from zero at Expedia. It now spans 50+ jurisdictions and protects ~$2B in annual booking value. Diagnose before redesigning. Automate before adding headcount. Build enough succession depth that the function doesn't depend on one person.

That includes the communication side of the work: I have built the partner outreach and communication playbooks that trigger automatically before and after a product launch, not just the operational ones, and I present regularly at senior leadership monthly business reviews and sit in Product's own monthly reviews as a key participant. I lead a core team of 8, a mix of managers, engineers, and senior analysts. For close to a year I also ran a second function at the same time as Interim Head of Regulatory Compliance and Operations, which added 9 more senior managers and managers reporting to me on top of that.

Clarifying Context on Executive Ghostwriting

While I have not been required to act as a direct executive ghostwriter in my most recent operations roles, this is a core capability I built extensively during my time at PwC. There, I regularly authored strategic briefs, performance narratives, and operational updates on behalf of leadership for executive-level consumption.

To bridge this into my work with the SVP of Product, I intentionally accelerate the adaptation process by combining my foundational consulting experience with advanced AI tooling. By leveraging AI to securely ingest and analyze the SVP’s past scripts, presentations, and written updates, I can rapidly deconstruct their natural syntax, preferred framing devices, and communication style. This allows me to scale up high-fidelity speechwriting and board-level drafting far more quickly than traditional observation alone, turning a historical onboarding ramp into an immediate operational asset.

One thing I'd want to be upfront about

The part of this role with the least direct evidence behind it is personal speechwriting for an executive audience and authoring board level materials. I present regularly at senior leadership monthly business reviews to executive sponsors and stakeholders, and I sit in Product's own monthly reviews as a key participant, so executive facing communication itself is not new to me. What is new is writing on someone else's behalf for an external audience: an all hands script, a public talk, a board page. My plan treats that gap on purpose: a smaller written piece with PR support by day 90, not a board page on day one. I would rather build that muscle in the open than overstate it here.
